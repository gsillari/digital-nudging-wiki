# EAST Framework

## Core idea

The [[EAST Framework]] is a practical checklist for designing behavioral interventions: make the target behavior Easy, Attractive, Social, and Timely. It was developed by the [[Behavioural Insights Team]] as an accessible way to translate [[Behavioral Economics]] and [[Choice Architecture]] into intervention design.

In this vault, EAST is the quick-reference page for the characteristics of good behavioral interventions.

## Quick reference

| Characteristic | Design question | Common tools | Digital examples |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Easy | What friction, confusion, or effort prevents action? | [[Defaults]], simplification, fewer steps, autofill, clearer instructions, reduced [[Sludge]] | One-click enrollment, short forms, default privacy choices that users can change, prefilled applications |
| Attractive | What will draw attention and make the action feel worthwhile? | Salience, framing, personalization, visual cues, incentives, gamification, feedback | Highlighted next action, personalized reminders, progress indicators, salient savings or risk information |
| Social | What do relevant others do, expect, or model? | Descriptive norms, injunctive norms, peer comparison, network seeding, reciprocity, operational transparency | "Most users like you completed this," visible participation, referral flows, peer commitments |
| Timely | When is the user most receptive or able to act? | Just-in-time prompts, fresh-start moments, immediate rewards, delayed costs, planning prompts, implementation intentions | Appointment reminders, onboarding nudges, renewal warnings, plan-making chatbots, timed notifications |

## Key distinctions

- EAST vs full methodology: EAST helps generate and refine intervention ideas, but it does not replace problem diagnosis, fieldwork, co-design, or evaluation.
- Easy vs coercive: making the target behavior easy is not the same as removing meaningful alternatives.
- Attractive vs manipulative: attention and framing should be user-serving and publicly defensible.
- Social proof vs social backfire: norm messages can backfire if they reveal that undesirable behavior is common or invoke the wrong reference group.
- Timely vs intrusive: a well-timed prompt helps at the moment of action; a poorly timed prompt becomes noise or pressure.

## Evidence and debate

[[Behavioural Insights Team, 2024|EAST: Four Simple Ways to Apply Behavioural Insights]] synthesizes a decade of behavioral intervention examples and updated evidence. Its strongest contribution for the course is not any single case study, but the organizing heuristic: interventions often work by reducing effort, increasing salience, using social context, and aligning with moments when people can act.

The report is careful about evidence. It notes replication issues, behavioral pitfalls, and the need to test interventions in context. That makes EAST useful as a starting framework, but not as a guarantee that a given intervention will work.

## Practical or policy relevance

For digital nudging, EAST can be used as a fast design review:

- Is the desired action easier than the alternatives?
- Is the relevant information salient, understandable, and well framed?
- Are social cues accurate, relevant, and unlikely to shame or backfire?
- Does the intervention arrive when the user can actually act?
- Have likely failure modes, user burdens, and unintended consequences been tested?
