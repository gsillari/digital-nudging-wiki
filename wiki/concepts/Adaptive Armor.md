# Adaptive Armor

## Core idea

[[Adaptive Armor]] is Ludwig, Mullainathan, Pink, and Rambachan's term for algorithms that help users navigate dynamic and adversarial choice environments. The image is defensive: when firms adapt their obfuscation, shrouded attributes, or confusing designs, user-serving algorithms can adapt too.

## Mechanism

Adaptive armor works by reading, comparing, translating, and reformatting complex choice environments in real time. Instead of relying only on static disclosures or one-time regulations, an algorithmic tool can surface the material information that a firm has buried or reframed.

The paper's main example is credit-card offers. If a provider highlights rewards while hiding fees or repayment penalties, an LLM-like tool can summarize the key tradeoffs and put the dangerous terms at the top.

## Digital relevance

Adaptive armor is a constructive answer to [[Sludge]] and [[Dark Patterns]]. It turns algorithmic adaptation into a user-serving capacity rather than a platform-serving one.

It is also a bridge between [[Smart Disclosure]] and [[Behavioral Economics 2.0]]. Smart disclosure gives tools machine-readable data; adaptive armor uses algorithmic interpretation to protect users when data or terms are confusing, adversarial, or strategically presented.
