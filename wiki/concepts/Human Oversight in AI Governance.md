# Human Oversight in AI Governance

## Core idea

[[Human Oversight in AI Governance]] is the requirement that responsible people remain able to guide, inspect, contest, correct, and justify AI systems. In this cluster, oversight is not a vague human-in-the-loop slogan. It has to be placed in the lifecycle: training, deployment, monitoring, auditing, explanation, escalation, and revision.

[[Coglianese and Crum, 2025a|Leashes, Not Guardrails]] gives the image: AI can move, adapt, and explore, but the leash only works if humans keep a firm grip on the handle.

## Forms of oversight

- Human-guided training: human judgment shapes development before deployment.
- Auditing: humans or institutions inspect whether systems perform as intended.
- Documentation and disclosure: humans can reconstruct objectives, data, tests, and decisions.
- Human-AI teaming: users or officials work with systems rather than merely rubber-stamping them.
- Escalation and contestation: affected people can reach a human when automated treatment is wrong, harmful, or insufficiently responsive.
- Empathic administration: human care remains part of institutional legitimacy, especially in high-stakes public systems.

## Digital relevance

Digital nudging often works through automation that feels frictionless precisely because human responsibility disappears from view. Oversight asks who chose the objective, who approved the experiment, who can see unequal effects, who can stop the system, and who answers to affected users.

For the course, this concept connects ethics to operations. A digital nudge is not responsibly governed just because a human approved it once. Oversight must remain active as the system learns, targets, tests, and changes.
