# Human-Guided AI Training

## Core idea

[[Human-Guided AI Training]] is the idea that human oversight should shape how AI systems are trained and developed, not merely how they are monitored after deployment. Humans can contribute domain judgment, salience, normative constraints, examples of acceptable and unacceptable reasoning, error diagnosis, and evaluation standards.

[[Coglianese and Crum, 2024|Taking Training Seriously]] treats human-guided training as a natural consequence of [[Management-Based AI Regulation]]. If organizations are legally required to manage AI risks, then one of the most important risk-management points is upstream in the training pipeline.

## Why training matters

- Training choices shape what the model learns to optimize, ignore, explain, and reproduce.
- Data-only training can encode historical artifacts, proxy variables, spurious correlations, or socially unacceptable patterns.
- Human guidance can help align models with human-recognizable concepts, reasons, and errors.
- Explainability can improve when systems are trained around human-salient features or feedback.
- Fairness work is easier when risk signals are incorporated during development rather than patched after deployment.

## Digital relevance

Digital nudging systems often learn from engagement, conversion, retention, complaint rates, and short-term behavior. Human-guided training asks whether those signals are enough. A recommender or AI assistant may optimize behavior while missing autonomy, welfare, dignity, context, or downstream harm.

For the course, this concept becomes a design principle: governance of digital choice architecture should inspect the training objective, training data, feedback signals, human review process, and evaluation criteria before the system is allowed to steer users at scale.
