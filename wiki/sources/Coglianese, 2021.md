# Coglianese, 2021: Administrative Law in the Automated State

## Summary

[[Coglianese, 2021|Administrative Law in the Automated State]] imagines a future in which public agencies increasingly rely on machine learning and automated systems. Coglianese argues that U.S. administrative law can accommodate this shift and may even find some of its core values strengthened by automation.

The constructive claim is that algorithmic administration can improve expertise, consistency, and accountability when systems are designed around specified public goals, validated carefully, and integrated into lawful administrative processes. The caution is that an automated state can become less empathic. Even if automated systems are accurate and legally defensible, government must still preserve the human care, responsiveness, and legitimacy that people expect from public institutions.

## Key claims

- Administrative law is flexible enough to accommodate responsible automation.
- Machine learning can advance expert administration by improving prediction, consistency, and evidence-based decision-making.
- Automation can also make accountability more explicit by hardwiring public objectives and reducing hidden discretion.
- Agencies must still explain purposes, design choices, validation efforts, and system operation.
- The greatest residual challenge is not only legality or accuracy, but empathy.
- An automated state must remain responsive to people's experiences, circumstances, dignity, and need to be heard.

## Course use

Use this paper as the normative endpoint of the administrative-law cluster. [[Coglianese and Lehr, 2017|Regulating by Robot]] and [[Coglianese and Lehr, 2019|Transparency and Algorithmic Governance]] defend legal compatibility; this paper asks what kind of automated state would deserve trust.

It is also a useful bridge back to behavioral public policy. Nudging is already concerned with how institutions meet people as boundedly rational, attention-limited, and situated agents. The automated state must not optimize away that human condition.

## Why it matters for Digital Nudging

Digital nudging can make public and private systems more expert, adaptive, and efficient. But it can also feel cold, manipulative, or impossible to contest. The paper gives the course an important legitimacy criterion: digital systems should not merely be accurate, efficient, and transparent enough for legal review. They should preserve meaningful human responsiveness.

For AI assistants, public-service chatbots, recommender systems, and automated eligibility tools, this means empathy is not decorative. It is part of governance.
