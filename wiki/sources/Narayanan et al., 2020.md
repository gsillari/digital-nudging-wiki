# Narayanan et al., 2020: Dark Patterns

## Summary

[[Narayanan et al., 2020|Dark Patterns: Past, Present, and Future]] is the best compact overview for placing dark patterns in the history of digital nudging. The article argues that contemporary dark patterns emerged from the convergence of three strands: retail deception and manipulation, behavioral economics and nudging, and Silicon Valley growth hacking.

The paper is especially important for the user's framing because it treats dark patterns as a subversion of nudging. Behavioral insights that were originally defended as helping people make better choices can be repurposed by firms to maximize revenue, data extraction, and engagement.

## Key claims

- Dark patterns are not merely old tricks placed on screens. Digital systems make them easier to test, scale, personalize, and automate.
- Behavioral economics supplies an understanding of cognitive biases, heuristics, defaults, social proof, scarcity, and framing. In dark patterns, those tools are used adversarially.
- Growth hacking supplies the optimization culture: interfaces are continuously tested against growth, conversion, retention, and revenue metrics.
- A/B testing converts broad behavioral principles into precise operational designs by discovering which wording, timing, colors, and flows work best.
- Dark patterns extract money, data, and attention.
- Dark patterns can be offered as third-party services, making manipulation modular and reusable.

## The historical convergence

The article's most useful contribution is the three-part genealogy:

- Retail deception provides the older market-manipulation repertoire: hidden fees, misleading claims, bait-and-switch tactics, and pressure selling.
- Behavioral nudging supplies the scientific and design vocabulary for shaping choices without removing options.
- Growth hacking supplies the platform-management objective and experimental infrastructure.

That convergence explains why digital dark patterns are structurally different from isolated deception. They are optimized choice architectures embedded in live systems.

## Why it matters for Digital Nudging

This source gives the course a clean formulation of the dark-pattern pivot: digital nudging can become adversarial when the same behavioral machinery is aimed at extraction rather than user welfare.

It also connects dark patterns to [[Continuous Optimization and Experimentation]]. The ethical issue is not only that a single screen is manipulative. It is that firms can run experiments until manipulation becomes subtle enough, profitable enough, or personalized enough to escape ordinary user resistance.
