Digital Nudging Wiki

This is the working knowledge base for the course Digital Nudging.

The first foundational source ingested is Nudge: The Final Edition, with selective unit notes for the chapters most useful to the course's opening vocabulary. The first practitioner quick reference is EAST Framework. The first dedicated ethics framework is The Ethics of Influence: Government in the Age of Behavioral Science, complemented by Nudge and the Manipulation of Choice for nudge definition and manipulation typology, and now synthesized in Nudging and the Ethics of Nudging. Hypernudge: Big Data as a Mode of Regulation by Design is the first cornerstone digital-nudging source on structural risks, Algorithms as a Vehicle to Reflective Equilibrium: Behavioral Economics 2.0 is the first programmatic source on constructive algorithmic support, and Personalized Nudging (Mills) supplies the delivery-versus-target personalization distinction. The dark-pattern branch is anchored by The Dark (Patterns) Side of UX Design, Dark Patterns at Scale, Dark Patterns: Past, Present, and Future, What Makes a Dark Pattern... Dark?, Shining a Light on Dark Patterns, and An Ontology of Dark Patterns Knowledge, with Dark Patterns as Adversarial Choice Architecture as the synthesis. The algorithmic fairness branch now begins with Machine Bias, Inherent Trade-Offs in the Fair Determination of Risk Scores, On Statistical Criteria of Algorithmic Fairness, Measuring Algorithmic Fairness, Fairness in Machine Learning: Lessons from Political Philosophy, Algorithmic Decision Making and the Cost of Fairness, and What If Algorithmic Fairness Is a Category Error?. The efficiency branch is anchored by Human Decisions and Machine Predictions and Fragile Algorithms and Fallible Decision-Makers, with Economics in the Age of Algorithms as the summary frame. The algorithmic-governance cluster adds Regulating by Robot, Transparency and Algorithmic Governance, Administrative Law in the Automated State, Taking Training Seriously, Regulating Multifunctionality, and Leashes, Not Guardrails as the administrative-law and management-based-regulation layer.
