# Digital Nudging Wiki

Digital environments do not merely present options: they organize attention, effort, expectations, and opportunity. Defaults, rankings, recommendations, consent flows, and AI assistants are forms of choice architecture. This wiki asks when that influence helps people, when it exploits them, and how it should be governed.

The reading route begins with nudging and its ethical limits, then examines dark patterns as a threat to meaningful choice. AI as choice aid explores the constructive alternative: systems that help users understand options and act on considered preferences. Predictive algorithms introduce a further problem: better forecasts can improve decisions while distributing errors and opportunities unequally. Governance brings these questions together at the institutional level, asking who sets objectives, tests systems, explains decisions, and answers for harm. Across all five threads, changing behavior is not enough. The harder questions concern welfare, agency, fairness, evidence, and the responsibilities of those who design the environment.

## How to use this wiki

This is a course-building wiki, not a finished textbook. The synthesis pages give the argument at lecture scale; concept pages define reusable vocabulary; source notes preserve what each paper or book contributes; unit notes turn larger sources into teachable chunks. The concept layer has been deliberately compressed: many useful subterms now live as sections or aliases inside the larger concepts rather than as separate pages.

Follow the route above for a first reading, or enter through a concept and follow its source links to examine the evidence. The [[Digital Nudging]] hub and [full catalog](catalog.md) retain the wider map; the links here are starting points, not the complete inventory.
