# Behavioural Insights Team, 2024: EAST: Four Simple Ways to Apply Behavioural Insights

## Summary

[[Behavioural Insights Team, 2024|EAST: Four Simple Ways to Apply Behavioural Insights]] is a revised and updated [[Behavioural Insights Team]] handbook for designing practical behavioral interventions. It condenses intervention design into four characteristics: make the target behavior Easy, Attractive, Social, and Timely. The report is deliberately pragmatic: it is meant to help practitioners generate and refine ideas, not to replace diagnosis, fieldwork, co-design, or evaluation.

For this vault, the source is best used as a quick reference for the properties of effective behavioral interventions. It complements [[Thaler and Sunstein, 2021|Nudge: The Final Edition]] by translating [[Nudge|nudging]] and [[Choice Architecture]] into an applied checklist.

## Key claims

- Effective interventions should reduce cognitive and practical effort. Defaults, fewer steps, simpler messages, and redesigned forms can have large effects because small friction costs often suppress action.
- Effective interventions should attract attention and frame behavior carefully. Salience, personalization, timely prompts, environmental cues, and well-designed incentives can change what people notice and how they interpret a choice.
- Effective interventions should use social forces with care. Descriptive norms, injunctive norms, networks, reciprocity, and operational transparency can help, but social information can backfire when the reference group is wrong or the norm reveals that undesirable behavior is common.
- Effective interventions should meet people at the right moment. Timing matters across the day, week, life course, and moments of disruption. Good interventions consider immediate costs and benefits and help people form concrete plans.
- EAST is not a complete methodology. The report explicitly says applied behavioral science also requires understanding context, testing assumptions, and using robust evaluation where possible.
- The 2024 revision is more cautious than a simple greatest-hits list: it flags replication problems, failed effects, and behavioral pitfalls.

## Evidence and methods

The handbook synthesizes behavioral science evidence, field experiments, and applied case studies. It emphasizes randomized controlled trials when feasible, but also notes that RCTs are not the only evaluation option.

The report's recurring methodological advice is to study the behavioral context before designing the intervention. BIT recommends fieldwork, attention to user experience, co-design with people who deliver and use services, and evaluation of whether an intervention works in the actual setting.

Selected examples useful for teaching:

- Easy: workplace pension auto-enrollment, green energy defaults, quick enrollment, simpler court summons forms, and university application assistance.
- Attractive: salient prompts, framing, built-environment cues, commitment savings products, deposit contracts, prosocial incentives, and gamification.
- Social: norm messages, peer comparison, network seeding, reciprocity in organ donation messages, and operational transparency.
- Timely: Ramadan diabetes screening, fresh-start timing, Save More Tomorrow, implementation intentions, and jobseeker action planning.

## Why it matters for Digital Nudging

EAST is immediately usable for digital nudging because most digital interventions can be checked against its four dimensions. Interfaces can make action easy through defaults, one-click flows, autofill, plain language, and reduced [[Sludge]]. They can make action attractive through salience, personalization, framing, and feedback. They can make action social through norm information, reputation cues, sharing, visible participation, and network effects. They can make action timely through notifications, reminders, fresh-start moments, just-in-time prompts, and planning tools.

The report is also a useful guardrail. It cautions that behavioral ideas do not travel automatically across contexts, that social norms and incentives can backfire, and that some once-promising effects fail to replicate. For digital nudging, that means EAST should be treated as a design checklist plus an evaluation prompt, not as a recipe book.

## Open questions

- How should EAST be adapted for personalized, AI-mediated, or recommender-driven interventions?
- When does making a behavior easy become insufficiently deliberative, especially for privacy, consent, or high-stakes decisions?
- Which EAST interventions remain effective when users recognize them as nudges or encounter them repeatedly?
