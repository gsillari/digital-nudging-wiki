# Gabaix and Laibson, 2006: Shrouded Attributes, Consumer Myopia, and Information Suppression in Competitive Markets

## Summary

[[Gabaix and Laibson, 2006|Shrouded Attributes, Consumer Myopia, and Information Suppression in Competitive Markets]] gives the economic mechanism behind hidden fees, add-ons, and strategically obscured product attributes. In a fully rational market, consumers infer that hidden add-on prices are likely to be high, so firms should have incentives to disclose. But if some consumers are myopic or unaware, firms can profit by advertising a cheap base good while shrouding expensive add-ons.

The striking result is that competition does not automatically solve the problem. Educating customers can turn them into sophisticated consumers who exploit the low base price while avoiding the add-on. That creates a "curse of debiasing": a firm may not profit from educating consumers, because the educated consumer can take the knowledge to the competitor's loss-leader offer.

## Key claims

- Firms exploit consumer mistakes when those mistakes are profitable.
- Shrouded attributes are product features, add-ons, fees, or penalties that consumers fail to consider at the moment of base-good choice.
- In markets with enough myopic consumers, firms can sustain high add-on prices and low base-good prices.
- Sophisticated consumers can benefit from shrouding by taking the cheap base good and avoiding the add-on.
- Competition may fail to debias consumers because unshrouding helps consumers generally rather than the unshrouding firm specifically.
- Shrouding can persist even with costless advertising and highly competitive base-good markets.
- Policy responses include mandatory unshrouding, warnings, increased add-on competition, and markup caps.

## Evidence and methods

The article develops formal models of discrete and continuous add-on demand. Examples include banking fees, hotel add-ons, printer cartridges, credit cards, mutual funds, and other markets where the advertised base price omits important later costs.

The paper also offers diagnostics for shrouding: consumer ignorance at the point of purchase, difficulty finding add-on prices, loss-leader base prices paired with high add-on markups, and learning patterns in which consumers reduce add-on demand after being charged.

## Why it matters for Digital Nudging

Shrouded attributes are one of the clearest bridges from behavioral economics to digital choice architecture. Digital interfaces can shroud fees, renewals, privacy costs, penalties, add-on prices, cancellation burdens, and long-run consequences while keeping formal choice intact.

For [[AI as Choice Aid]], the paper supplies the adversarial market problem. If firms profit from consumer myopia, a user-serving tool must do more than present options. It has to surface hidden costs, translate complex terms, and sometimes counteract strategic obfuscation. This is exactly the role later described as adaptive armor ([[Algorithmic Thought Partners]]).

It also explains why disclosure and competition may be insufficient. A market can remain exploitative even when consumers could, in principle, learn the relevant information.
