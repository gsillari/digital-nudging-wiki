# Management-Based AI Regulation

## Core idea

[[Management-Based AI Regulation]] governs AI by requiring organizations to create and maintain internal systems for identifying, assessing, mitigating, documenting, auditing, and revising risks. It is especially useful when regulators cannot sensibly prescribe every permitted design choice or output in advance.

In the Coglianese-Crum cluster, management-based regulation is the answer to [[AI Heterogeneity and Multifunctionality]]. Because AI systems are dynamic, context-sensitive, and often multifunctional, rigid "guardrails" are too blunt. A better regulatory model is a flexible "leash": developers and deployers retain room to innovate, but humans must keep an active grip on risk management.

## Key elements

- Risk identification: organizations must define what harms the AI system could produce in specific contexts.
- Risk assessment: organizations must evaluate likelihood, severity, distribution, and uncertainty.
- Technical and managerial controls: organizations must use training, testing, documentation, monitoring, and escalation procedures to reduce risks.
- Human oversight: responsible humans must remain involved across the lifecycle.
- Auditing and validation: regulators, third parties, or internal reviewers need evidence that the management system is not merely pro forma.
- Continuous revision: controls must update as the model, users, deployment contexts, and risks change.

## Digital relevance

Digital nudging systems are hard to regulate only by interface rules because ranking, personalization, defaults, recommendation, and experimentation change continuously. Management-based regulation asks a different question: does the platform have an accountable process for deciding what behavioral objectives are legitimate, what risks are monitored, what experiments are allowed, what harms trigger intervention, and what evidence is preserved?

For the course, this concept becomes a governance bridge between [[Hypernudge]] and constructive algorithmic support. Hypernudges create opacity and continuous modification; management-based regulation supplies one institutional response.
