# Personalized Nudging

## Core idea

[[Personalized Nudging]] uses data about individual or group heterogeneity to adapt nudges. The point is to avoid one-size-fits-all interventions when people differ in circumstances, preferences, biases, constraints, or responsiveness to specific nudge mechanisms.

Mills's key contribution in [[Mills, 2022|Personalized Nudging (Mills)]] is to distinguish two forms of personalization: content or target personalization, which he calls choice personalization, and delivery personalization.

## Two dimensions

- Content or target personalization: the system personalizes what the user is nudged toward. Mills calls this choice personalization. The nudge mechanism is already selected, and data are used to determine the best target, option, default, date, contribution rate, or outcome for this person.
- Delivery personalization: the system personalizes how the nudge is delivered. Data are used to select the nudge mechanism, timing, format, channel, frame, or salience strategy most likely to work for this person.

The difference is crucial. Delivery personalization asks how to influence. Content or target personalization asks what direction the influence should take.

## Examples

- Choice/content personalization: a savings default differs by user because some people under-save and others over-save.
- Choice/content personalization: a fresh-start nudge uses a personally meaningful date, such as a birthday or anniversary.
- Delivery personalization: one person receives a default, another receives a social-norm message, and another receives a reminder because they respond differently to nudge types.
- Combined personalization: a platform chooses both the medium of an intervention and the product, option, or behavior promoted through it.

## Relationship to adjacent concepts

[[Precision Nudging]] often focuses on improving the precision of delivery: the right message, timing, or nudge type for a target behavior. Personalized nudging is broader because it also includes content or target personalization.

[[Behavioral Economics 2.0]] goes further. It asks not merely how to personalize the nudge or target, but how an algorithmic tool can help determine what the user would choose under [[Reflective Equilibrium]].

[[Hypernudge]] is the risk-side cousin of personalized nudging: personalization plus data, continuous feedback, and opacity can become structural behavioral control.

## Governance issues

Personalized nudging raises distinctive governance questions:

- What data are needed to capture relevant heterogeneity?
- Does personalization require Big Data, or can simpler data suffice?
- Who holds the relevant data, and can public actors access it?
- Does personalization undermine universality, shared experience, equal treatment, or public scrutiny?
- Are users able to know why they received a different nudge or target from others?
- What privacy, stewardship, and accountability duties attach to the choice architect?
