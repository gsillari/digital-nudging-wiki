# Shrouded Attributes

## Core idea

[[Shrouded Attributes]] are product or service features that are important for welfare but hidden, delayed, de-emphasized, or made difficult to evaluate at the moment of choice. Typical examples include add-on prices, fees, penalties, renewal terms, usage costs, privacy costs, and cancellation burdens.

The basic market failure is that shrouding can persist even under competition. If some consumers are myopic or unaware, firms can advertise a cheap base good while profiting from expensive add-ons. [[Gabaix and Laibson, 2006|Shrouded Attributes, Consumer Myopia, and Information Suppression in Competitive Markets]] is the anchor for that mechanism.

## Key distinctions

- Hidden information vs shrouded information: shrouding need not make information impossible to find; it makes it practically absent from the choice moment.
- Base good vs add-on: the advertised product may be cheap while later complementary costs are high.
- Myopic consumers vs sophisticated consumers: myopes ignore add-ons; sophisticates anticipate and avoid them.
- Shrouding vs sludge: shrouding hides or de-emphasizes attributes; sludge makes action costly. Many digital designs use both.
- Competition vs debiasing: competition may not reveal shrouded terms because educating consumers can fail to benefit the educating firm.

## Evidence and debate

The "curse of debiasing" explains why firms may not compete by educating consumers. A consumer educated about hidden add-ons can take advantage of another firm's low base price while avoiding add-ons, so the educating firm may not win the customer. Gabaix and Laibson supply this explanation.

The disclosure problem is that making information technically available does not guarantee that users can use it. Disclosure can fail when attention, motivation, complexity, or overload prevent effective uptake, as [[Loewenstein et al., 2014|Disclosure: Psychology Changes Everything]] emphasizes.

The choice-aid response is to make hidden attributes usable at the moment of decision. AI choice engines can surface total costs, translate terms, compare add-ons, and warn about hidden burdens. Sunstein's [[Sunstein, 2023|Behavioral Biases, Choice Engines, and Paternalistic AI]] treats shrouded attributes as a central reason to build and regulate such tools.

## Practical or policy relevance

Digital environments make shrouding easier to implement and test. A website can highlight a base price, hide fees until late in checkout, bury renewal terms, obscure privacy costs, or make cancellation consequences hard to see.

For [[AI as Choice Aid]], shrouded attributes are the canonical case for defensive assistance. A user-serving tool can surface total costs, translate terms, compare add-ons, and warn about hidden burdens. This is closely related to adaptive armor ([[Algorithmic Thought Partners]]).
