# Algorithmic Thought Partners

## Core idea

[[Algorithmic Thought Partners]] are AI or algorithmic systems that help people reason, understand, predict, compare, implement, and practice decisions. The point is to shift digital nudging from intervention design toward tool-building: not only arranging the user's environment, but helping the user think and act under better conditions. Ludwig and coauthors make this the central mechanism of [[Ludwig et al., 2025|Algorithms as a Vehicle to Reflective Equilibrium: Behavioral Economics 2.0]].

A thought partner does not simply choose for the user or push the user toward a preselected behavior. Its ideal role is to help the user approach reflective equilibrium.

The same idea extends across disclosure, insurance, shrouded attributes, recommendations, and engagement optimization. The common problem is that users often need help making sense of a choice, not merely a prediction of what they are likely to do. That broader application is developed in [[AI as Choice Aid]].

## Behavioral Economics 2.0

The former concept "Behavioral Economics 2.0" now lives here. Ludwig, Mullainathan, Pink, and Rambachan use the phrase to name a shift from nudges done to people toward algorithmic tools people can use. The point is not simply more precise nudging. It is a different paradigm: systems that help users understand tradeoffs, simulate consequences, practice decisions, implement chosen plans, and approach reflective equilibrium.

## Reflective equilibrium

Reflective equilibrium is the target state for a good thought partner. The system should help users bring stated values, concrete choices, emotions, constraints, and consequences into better alignment. It should not infer welfare only from clicks, purchases, engagement, or compliance.

## Adaptive armor

Adaptive armor is the defensive version of algorithmic thought partnership. When firms shroud fees, complicate terms, or create confusing interfaces, a user-serving AI can translate, compare, flag risks, and surface hidden attributes. It is especially relevant to [[Shrouded Attributes]], [[Smart Disclosure]], [[Sludge]], and [[Dark Patterns]].

Sunstein's [[Sunstein, 2023|Behavioral Biases, Choice Engines, and Paternalistic AI]] gives the closest legal-policy version of this idea: AI choice engines can help users navigate complexity and bias, provided they remain answerable to chooser welfare rather than platform objectives.

## Functions

- Explanation: translate confusing forms, instructions, terms, or consequences.
- Information: add forecasts, comparisons, and missing context that the user could not easily process alone.
- Reflection: identify biases or psychological traps that may distort judgment.
- Implementation: help carry out a chosen plan, such as filling forms or setting future defaults.
- Practice: create repeated opportunities for rehearsal, feedback, and debiasing.

## Contrast with recommendation systems

Traditional [[Recommendation Systems]] often predict what a user would choose or what similar users chose. Thought partners should instead help with what the user would choose under better information and considered judgment.

That distinction matters because observed behavior can encode mistakes. A recommender that predicts mistakes well may amplify them. A thought partner should help users reason through them.

## Digital relevance

Examples include LLM explanations of court summonses, AI financial advisors, credit-card comparison tools, sunk-cost alerts, pretrial decision aids, AI agents that implement chosen actions, police training simulators, and teacher feedback systems.

The course should treat thought partners as one of the constructive frontiers of digital nudging: digital systems that enlarge agency rather than shrink it.
