# Algorithmic Fairness

## Scope

[[Algorithmic Fairness]] organizes the course material on bias, parity, prediction, efficiency, transparency, and institutional accountability. It belongs in a digital nudging course because rankings, scores, feeds, recommendation systems, fraud screens, AI assistants, and eligibility tools all shape choice environments before anyone reaches a final decision.

## Course role

The module begins with [[COMPAS Controversy]] because it makes the stakes concrete: predictive systems can steer human judgment while distributing error burdens unequally. [[Fairness Impossibility Results]] then explain why calibration, [[Predictive Parity]], and [[Error Rate Parity]] cannot usually be satisfied together when base rates differ and predictions are imperfect. From there, the course asks how fairness should be justified: by evidential meaning, legal consequences, political philosophy, regulatory goals, sociotechnical system design, or efficiency gains.

For the full argument, use [[Predictive Algorithms, Bias, and Fairness]].
